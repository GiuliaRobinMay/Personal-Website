Sound familiar?

Your platform gives every member the same room. Your members are not the same.

You did the hard part. The tribe exists, people joined, the numbers look fine on paper. And still the space feels like a broadcast channel instead of a home.

  • Everyone lands in the same feed

    The brand-new member and your five-year veteran open the identical screen, and neither one finds the thing meant for them.

  • You are the personalisation

    Every welcome, nudge and check-in runs through you by hand. That works at fifty members. It quietly breaks at five hundred.

  • Your tools do not talk to each other

    The community, the email list, the course platform, the CRM, the calendar. Five systems, and you are the integration between them.

  • Off-the-shelf stops where you get interesting

    The features you actually need are the ones no platform ships, so you flatten your idea to fit the software.

What it costs

A community does not die loudly. It goes quiet. Members stop opening the app, then stop renewing, and you are left running a room that looks full and feels empty.

The fix is not more posting. It is giving people something built for them, waiting when they arrive.

What it looks like instead

Members log in and the space knows them. Where they left off, what is on today, what comes next. Behind the glass, the systems talk to each other and the busywork runs itself.

The flag I am planting

Six things I believe, and one of them is heresy.

  1. 01

    A feed is not a community. It is a queue. Nobody ever belonged to a queue.

  2. 02

    Software should know who just walked in. If it treats the day-one member and the five-year veteran identically, it is not software, it is furniture.

  3. 03

    A hundred people who would miss you beats a hundred thousand who would not. Build for the smallest tribe that can sustain you, then build them something absurdly specific.

  4. 04

    If you can describe it, it can be built. Not next quarter. This week. That sentence was a lie for twenty-five years and it is true now.

  5. 05

    Vibe coding is not a shortcut. It is a superpower — in trained hands. This is the heresy. Half the industry says it makes fragile toys. They are right, if you have never shipped software. I have shipped it for twenty-five years. The AI changed my speed. It did not change my standards.

  6. 06

    Custom should be the default, not the luxury. The reason communities all feel the same is that building anything used to cost a quarter and a developer. That reason is gone.

Why me, and why now

25 years of building applications. 10 of building communities. The tools finally let me do both at once.

For most of my career I built custom applications for companies. UX, human-centred design, real software for real users, with all the unglamorous parts: edge cases, accessibility, the thing that happens when someone clicks the button twice.

Then I moved into community, and hundreds of tribes later I kept hitting the same wall. The thing the host needed did not exist, and no platform was going to build it. I could design it, I could even build it, but not at a price or a speed that made sense for a community.

That wall is gone. Vibe coding collapsed the distance between describing a thing and running it. For someone who has never built software, that is a fast way to make something fragile. For someone who spent twenty-five years learning what good looks like, it is the best tool ever handed to me.

The things people actually ask

Including the awkward one.

Hold on. Is “vibe coding” not just AI writing sloppy code?

In untrained hands, yes, often. Someone who has never shipped software cannot tell the difference between code that works and code that works today. They find out when it breaks in front of five hundred members.

I spent twenty-five years as a custom application developer before any of this existed. I know what accessibility means, what happens on a bad connection, what a data model should look like, and which corners you are allowed to cut. The AI made me faster. It did not make me less careful. That is the whole difference, and it is the reason this is worth paying for.

I am on Mighty Networks / Circle / Skool / Kajabi. Does this work there?

Yes. Everything I build is a real web application that gets embedded inside your community, so it works anywhere you can place an embed — which is all of them. Your members never leave. It carries your colours, your fonts and your language, so it does not read as a bolt-on.

Mighty Networks is where I have done the most of it, so if that is where you are, we skip a lot of discovery.

How long does it actually take?

A single focused piece — a daily menu, an assessment, an onboarding flow — is days. Something with several moving parts, accounts, automations and integrations behind it, is a few weeks. You see a working version long before it is finished, which is the point: you react to the real thing instead of approving a document.

What does it cost?

It is priced per build, not per hour, and you know the number before anything starts. Small, self-contained pieces sit at one end; a full custom member experience with an engine behind it sits at the other. The call is free and I will tell you honestly which end you are at — including if the answer is “a template already does this, save your money.”

Who owns it? Am I locked in?

You own it. The code is yours, it runs on your hosting or mine as you prefer, and if you want to hand it to another developer next year you can. I would rather earn the next project than trap you in this one.

What happens when it breaks, or I want to change something?

Things get tuned after launch — that is normal and it is included for the first stretch after it goes live. After that, most people keep me on a light retainer, because a community that is alive keeps wanting new things. There is no obligation to.

Can you build the course itself, not just the software around it?

Yes, and it is some of my favourite work. Structure, sequence, what a lesson is allowed to ask of someone, where people quit and how to stop them — that is community and instructional design, which is the job I did before this one. Bring a method that lives in your head and we will build both the course and the thing it runs in.

Do you only work with big communities?

Not only, but that is where this pays for itself fastest. Below a hundred members you can still be the personalisation yourself. Somewhere past a few hundred, doing it by hand stops working, and that is exactly the point where custom software becomes cheaper than your own time.
